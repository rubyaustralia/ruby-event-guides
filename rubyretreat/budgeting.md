## Budgeting

The budget is often the most daunting part of organising your first event. It's hard to know what you can (or should) reasonably spend, how many tickets you'll sell, and how to line everything up so you'll (ideally) break even.

### What can I afford?

The [venue](venue.md) is the largest expense you'll need to cover - finding an affordable, comfortable, correctly-sized venue that can accommodate the needs of your attendees is tricky. Figuring out the "affordable" part of that question means having a clear idea of what your budget might be, which in turn means working out what expenses you will have aside from the venue. Unfortunately, Retreat attendance is not yet back to pre-COVID levels so you will likely have a bit less to work with than in previous years.

Example ticket pricing and attendance from recent events:

* **Ruby Retreat Warrnambool, October 2024:** 59 tickets sold, $350 full price ticket. Attendance for this one was capped at 60 due to the size of the venue (it sold out, but there was one refund).
* **Rails Camp Adelaide, February 2022:** 90 tickets sold, $350 full price ticket. First post-COVID event.
* **Rails Camp Melbourne, November 2019:** 86 tickets sold, $310 full price ticket. A more typical pre-COVID, Melbourne-based event - Melbourne and Sydney events have historically sold better (and in that order).
* **Rails Camp Perth, May 2019:** 46 tickets sold, $300 full price ticket. Good attendance given the location.

Let's take Warrnambool as an example. After deducting GST ($31.82) the organiser's budget was $318.18 per head. A venue that charges $260 per head is potentially a good deal here, assuming that includes all meals - but if outside catering is needed, that changes the equation.

In this case there would be $58.18 per head left over, or about $3,490 in total. This seems like a lot, but there is still [transport](transport.md), [catering](catering.md), and potentially [swag](swag.md) to keep in mind.

If you're comfortable that you can charge around the $300-$350 mark, get enough attendees, and have enough left over to comfortably cover catering, transport, and other things that crop up - then your budget is probably in good shape.
